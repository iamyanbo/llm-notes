LLM Notes.

The purpose of these essays is to understand how language models compute and learn, and what is involved in putting those computations to use.

Foundations begins with language and develops attention and the transformer. Transformers in Practice examines changes to that construction, how its parameters are learned, and how generation is carried out. Miscellaneous Topics considers the hardware, experiments, and decisions surrounding these models.

The essays

01 · Foundations

Understanding attention and transformers

A reader can distinguish who acts and who is affected in a sentence, even when the same words occur in a different arrangement. A model needs a numerical representation in which those distinctions remain available. Beginning with this problem, the notes develop attention, the transformer, and the calculations used to train it and generate text.

02 · Architecture, learning, and generation

Transformers in Practice

The transformer in Foundations leaves many choices open: how position enters a comparison, how parameters are learned or adapted, and how generation is carried out on a machine. Each essay takes one of these questions and examines what a proposed construction changes, what it saves, and what it gives up. The essays can be read separately.

03 · Hardware, experiments, and use

Miscellaneous Topics

A matrix product in a training equation does not tell us how long the calculation will take or which machines we can afford to run it on. These essays examine the physical resources behind a model’s computation, the evidence used to judge its behavior, and the decisions involved in giving it tools and putting it to use. They need not be read in order.
